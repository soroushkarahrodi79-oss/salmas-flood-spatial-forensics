# Project scope and decision question

## Decision question

How far can open Earth observation, terrain analysis and time-stamped public evidence robustly reconstruct the 22 October 2026 Salmas flash flood and identify potentially exposed receptors without claiming precision that the observations do not support?

## Primary users

- geospatial and disaster-risk researchers;
- local and regional planners reviewing evidence needs;
- open-source humanitarian mapping practitioners;
- portfolio reviewers assessing reproducible spatial-analysis capability.

This is not an operational emergency-management product and has no institutional endorsement.

## In scope

- versioned event chronology and source reconciliation;
- affected-settlement geolocation with coordinate uncertainty;
- precipitation context at the native support of the chosen product;
- upstream catchments and terrain-supported runoff pathways;
- Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 observation-availability audit;
- conservative satellite-change mapping when valid pre/post pairs exist;
- potentially exposed buildings, transport infrastructure and agricultural land;
- explicit observation, model and source uncertainty;
- bilingual Persian–English public communication.

## Out of scope without new evidence

- exact maximum water extent;
- flood depth, velocity, discharge or hydrograph reconstruction;
- return periods or design-storm claims;
- parcel- or household-level damage;
- economic-loss estimates;
- climate-event attribution;
- official warnings or performance judgements;
- predictive early-warning deployment.

## Stop conditions

Satellite flood delineation must stop if no post-event acquisition observes a plausible residual signal, if the post scene is not geometrically comparable, or if terrain and land-cover artefacts cannot be separated from water-related change.

Exposure counting must stop if building or road completeness cannot be characterized. Counts from incomplete volunteered data must be labelled partial or withheld.

Hydraulic modelling remains prohibited until event hydrographs or defensible rainfall-runoff inputs, channel geometry, structures and calibration observations are available.

## Phase 1 interpretation boundary

Phase 1 maps modelled topographic susceptibility and observation feasibility. It does not map the flood, its water depth, its damaged buildings or a local design storm. Candidate catchments and transport crossings are hypotheses for field or higher-resolution validation. Any future event-evidence envelope must pass the sensor and exposure gates before it can support receptor counting.
